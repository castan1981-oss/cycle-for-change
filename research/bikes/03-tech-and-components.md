# Bike tech — how the machine works and what the words mean

Evergreen reference. Current-year launches are in

## 2. Frame materials

- **Steel**: 4130 chromoly (the default), Reynolds 531 (1935, the classic),
  853, 725; Columbus SL/SLX/Spirit; Tange (Japan). Repairable, comfortable,
  heavier. Custom builders and touring/bikepacking live here. TIG-welded,
  fillet-brazed, or lugged (the traditional look).
- **Aluminum**: 6061 (common) and 7005; Cannondale, Klein, Giant pioneered
  oversized fat tubes in the 80s–90s. Stiff, light, cheap, not repairable,
  finite fatigue life. Hydroformed tubes since the 2000s. CAAD, Allez,
  Émonda ALR are the best modern examples.
- **Titanium**: 3Al-2.5V (tubes) or 6Al-4V (machined parts). Corrosion-proof,
  springy, lifetime frames, expensive, hard to weld. Moots, Litespeed, Lynskey,
  Seven, Mosaic, Bastion (3D-printed lugs), No. 22, Sage.
- **Carbon fiber**: since ~1986 (Look KG86, Kestrel 4000 the first monocoque
  1986/87, Trek OCLV 1992). Layups of unidirectional and woven prepreg in
  molds with internal bladders. Highest stiffness-to-weight, shapeable for
  aero, cracks rather than dents, repairable by specialists. "High modulus"
  vs "intermediate modulus" is marketing about fiber stiffness. Nearly all
  made in Taiwan/China; Time (RTM process) and Look are exceptions.
- **Bamboo / wood / magnesium / scandium**: niche or dead.

## 3. Geometry vocabulary

- **Stack / reach**: vertical and horizontal distance from BB center to the
  top of the head tube. The only honest way to compare fit across brands.
- **Head tube angle**: steeper (73–74°) = quick road handling; slacker
  (63–66°) = stable downhill MTB. Gravel ~70–72°.
- **Trail**: combination of head angle, fork offset and wheel size that sets
  steering feel. Road ~55–60 mm; MTB 100–130 mm.
- **Seat tube angle**: modern MTBs run steep (76–78°) to put the rider over
  the pedals on climbs.
- **Chainstay length**: short = playful/quick; long = stable, more tire room.
- **Bottom bracket drop**: how far the BB sits below the axles. More drop =
  lower, more stable.
- **Wheelbase**: total; modern MTBs and gravel bikes have grown a lot.
- **Long, low, slack**: the 2010s MTB trend (Mondraker "Forward Geometry,"
  Pole, Geometron) that is now everywhere.
- **Compact geometry**: sloping top tube (Giant TCR, 1997, Mike Burrows).
- **Sizing**: road bikes by seat-tube cm (52, 54, 56); MTB and gravel by
  S/M/L; Trek and others by numbers. Nobody agrees.

## 4. Drivetrains

- **Speeds**: the count of rear cogs. Road is 12 (Shimano, SRAM) or 13
  (Campagnolo SR 13, SRAM XPLR 13, Shimano's 13-speed in development). MTB is
  12. Entry bikes 8–10. More speeds = smaller jumps, not a bigger range.
- **Chainrings**: 2x road (53/39 "standard," 52/36 "semi-compact," 50/34
  "compact," 48/35 SRAM). 1x on MTB (30–34T), gravel (38–44T), and some road.
  Sub-compact 46/30 for gravel/endurance. Front derailleur is dying outside
  road racing.
- **Cassettes**: road 11-28 to 11-34; gravel 10-44 to 10-52; MTB 10-51/52
  (Eagle, XTR). SRAM's 10T cog needs an **XDR/XD driver**; Shimano 12-speed
  MTB needs **Micro Spline**; classic is **HG (Hyperglide)**.
- **Electronic shifting**: Shimano **Di2** (wired to a battery in the seat
  post; 12-speed road is semi-wireless: wireless levers, wired derailleurs),
  SRAM **AXS** (fully wireless, swappable batteries on each derailleur),
  Campagnolo **EPS** (wired, now **Super Record Wireless**). Mechanical is
  now only 105 and below, GRX, and Campagnolo Chorus/Ekar.
- **SRAM Transmission / "T-type"** (2023): derailleur bolts straight to a
  **UDH** (Universal Derailleur Hanger) frame, no B-tension, no limit screws,
  shifts under load. Now on road as **Red/Force/Rival XPLR 13** and DH.
- **Gearboxes and hubs**: Pinion (frame gearbox), Rohloff (14-sp hub),
  Shimano Alfine/Nexus (8/11-sp hubs), Enviolo (CVT), Classified Powershift
  (2-sp in the rear hub, replaces a front derailleur). Belt drive (Gates)
  pairs with these; no derailleur, no grease.
- **Cranks and bottom brackets**: the standards mess. **BSA/English threaded
  68 mm** (the good one; Trek and Specialized returned to it, **T47** is the
  oversized threaded version), **BB30 / PF30 / BB86 / BB92 / BB386EVO /
  BBright** press-fit variants (creak-prone). Crank spindles: Shimano 24 mm
  Hollowtech II, SRAM DUB 28.99 mm, 30 mm.
- **Chains**: KMC makes many for others. Waxing (Silca, Ceramic Speed, Molten
  Speed Wax) is the efficiency/cleanliness trend; drip lubes for everyone
  else. 12/13-speed chains are narrow and cross-brand compatibility is
  limited.
- **Crank length**: shorter cranks (160–165 mm) are the 2020s trend, driven
  by Pogačar (165 mm) and aero positions.
- **Pro gearing 2025–26**: 54/40 or 56/44 with 10-33 or 10-36; the UCI's
  attempted 54x11 gear cap was struck down in court in 2026 (SRAM v UCI).

## 5. Brakes, wheels, tires

- **Disc brakes**: flat-mount (road/gravel), post-mount (MTB). Rotor sizes
  140/160 mm road, 180/200/203/220 mm MTB. Shimano uses mineral oil, SRAM
  uses DOT fluid (Maven, Code, Level; Motive is SRAM's mineral-oil line).
  Rim brakes are legacy but loved (lighter, simpler).
- **Thru-axles**: 12x100 mm front / 12x142 mm rear (road, gravel), 15x110
  **Boost** front / 12x148 Boost rear (MTB), 12x157 **SuperBoost** (Pivot,
  Knolly). Quick-release is legacy.
- **Wheel sizes**: 700c = 29" = 622 mm bead seat (ISO/ETRTO). 650b = 27.5" =
  584 mm. 26" = 559 mm. **Mullet** = 29" front / 27.5" rear. 32" wheels are
  emerging on XC in 2026. 700c/650b "road plus" swap = bigger tire, same
  outer diameter.
- **Rim internal width**: road went 15 → 19 → 21 → 23–25 mm; gravel 25 mm;
  MTB 30 mm. Wider rims support wider tires at lower pressure.
- **Hookless (TSS)** rims: cheaper to make, popular with Zipp/Enve/Giant,
  limited to ~72 psi and specific tire pairings. Thomas De Gendt's 2024 crash
  ignited the debate; the trend in 2025–26 is back toward hooked or
  "mini-hook."
- **Tubeless**: sealant instead of tubes; MTB standard since ~2008, road
  mainstream since ~2020. Tubulars (sewn, glued) survive only in cyclocross
  and track. TPU tubes (Tubolito, Pirelli, Schwalbe Aerothan) are the light
  clincher alternative.
- **Tire width**: road 28 mm is the default in 2026, 30–32 mm common, pros on
  28–30. Lower pressure (50–70 psi) is faster on real roads because of
  impedance losses. Rolling resistance data: bicyclerollingresistance.com.
- **Rim depth**: aero benefit vs. crosswind stability; UCI capped mass-start
  rims at 65 mm from 2026.
- **Spokes**: J-bend vs straight-pull; Sapim, DT Swiss; carbon spokes on
  Chinese and some high-end wheels.

## 6. Contact points

- **Saddles**: short-nose (Specialized Power, 2015) took over. Cutouts,
  widths 130–168 mm by sit-bone measurement. 3D-printed padding (Fizik
  Adaptive, Specialized Mirror, Selle Italia 3D). Brooks leather for the
  patient.
- **Pedals**: **SPD** (2-bolt, MTB/gravel/commute, recessed cleat, walkable),
  **SPD-SL / Look Keo / Wahoo Speedplay** (3-bolt or 4-bolt road), Time ATAC,
  Crankbrothers Eggbeater. "Clipless" means clips-in (the name refers to
  ditching toe clips). Flats with pins for MTB.
- **Bars**: road width shrinking (36–40 cm, flared drops); UCI min 400 mm
  outside-to-outside from 2026 to stop 32 cm "puppy paws" bars. Integrated
  bar-stems hide cables and complicate fit. MTB bars 760–800 mm, rise 10–35
  mm. Gravel flare 12–25°.
- **Bike fit**: stack/reach, saddle height (~109% inseam to pedal spindle is
  the old LeMond formula; fitters use knee angle 140–150°), saddle setback,
  reach, drop. Retül (Specialized), Trek Precision Fit, bikefit.com. Short
  cranks and narrow bars are the 2020s fit story.

## 7. Suspension (MTB)

- **Fork travel**: 100 mm XC → 120–150 trail → 160–180 enduro → 200 DH
  (dual crown). Air springs (Fox Float, RockShox Pike/Lyrik/Zeb) vs coil.
- **Damping**: compression (high/low speed) and rebound. Fox GRIP2/GRIP X2,
  RockShox Charger 3. Fox Live Valve / RockShox Flight Attendant = electronic
  auto-adjusting.
- **Rear linkages**: single pivot, Horst link / four-bar (Specialized FSR
  patent, expired 2013), VPP (Santa Cruz; dropped on 2026 Tallboy), DW-link
  (Pivot, Ibis), Maestro (Giant), ABP (Trek), Switch Infinity (Yeti), high
  pivot with idler (Forbidden, Norco, DH bikes). Anti-squat, anti-rise,
  leverage curve, progression are the tuning words.
- **Dropper posts**: 100–240 mm, internally routed, the single biggest MTB
  usability change of the 2010s. Wireless (RockShox Reverb AXS).
- **Gravel suspension**: RockShox Rudy, Fox 32 TC, Lauf leaf-spring, Cannondale
  Lefty Oliver, Specialized Future Shock (in the stem). Divisive.

## 8. E-bikes

- **US classes** (model law, ~40 states): **Class 1** pedal-assist to 20 mph;
  **Class 2** throttle to 20 mph; **Class 3** pedal-assist to 28 mph. Motor
  cap 750 W nominal. Anything faster or more powerful is legally a moped or
  motorcycle, and the 2025–26 crackdown (California, NYC, Utah, many towns)
  targets "e-motos" (Sur-Ron, Talaria) and out-of-class Super73/Lectric-type
  bikes with unlockable modes.
- **EU**: EPAC = 250 W continuous, 25 km/h cutoff, pedal-assist only; "speed
  pedelec" 45 km/h needs registration, plate, helmet.
- **Motors**: **mid-drive** (Bosch Performance CX, Shimano EP801, Specialized
  Turbo Full Power 2.2 / Brose, Bafang M-series, DJI Avinox, TQ HPR50/60,
  Fazua Ride 60, Yamaha, Pinion MGU) vs **hub-drive** (cheaper, DTC bikes:
  Lectric, Rad, Aventon). Mid-drive uses the gears; hub doesn't. Torque
  (Nm) and peak watts are the spec-sheet numbers; "SL" / lightweight e-bikes
  run 35–60 Nm with 320–430 Wh batteries.
- **Batteries**: 400–800 Wh typical; range 20–100 miles depending on assist.
  Lithium-ion fires (mostly cheap, uncertified, damaged or aftermarket
  packs) drove **UL 2849** (system) and **UL 2271** (battery) certification
  laws (NYC 2023) and the 2026 federal CPSC rule-making.
- **Cargo**: front-loader (Urban Arrow, Bullitt, R&M Load) vs longtail
  (Tern GSD, Yuba, Rad Wagon, Specialized Globe Haul). The fastest-growing
  family-transport category in US cities.

## 9. Electronics, training, data

- **Power meters**: watts at the crank (Quarq, SRM, Power2Max, Stages,
  4iiii, Shimano), spider, pedal (Favero Assioma, Garmin Rally, Wahoo
  Powrlink), hub (PowerTap, dead). Left-only vs dual-sided.
- **FTP** (functional threshold power): roughly the highest power you can
  hold for an hour; the basis of training zones. **W/kg** for climbing.
  **Normalized power**, **TSS**, **CTL/ATL/TSB** (TrainingPeaks "Performance
  Management Chart"). Zone 2 / polarized training is the 2020s orthodoxy.
- **Heart rate**: chest strap (Garmin HRM, Polar H10, Wahoo Tickr) beats
  wrist optical. HRV for recovery (Whoop, Oura, Garmin).
- **Head units**: Garmin Edge (540/840/1050/850), Wahoo Elemnt (Bolt, Roam,
  Ace), Hammerhead Karoo, Coros Dura, Bryton, iGPSport. **Varia** rear radar
  is the safety accessory that actually changed behavior.
- **Software**: Strava (social, segments, KOM/QOM, Local Legends; the
  Cycle for Change tally reads its API), Zwift / Rouvy / MyWhoosh / TrainerRoad
  (indoor), TrainingPeaks / Intervals.icu (analysis), Komoot / Ride with GPS
  (routes), Garmin Connect / Wahoo app.
- **Smart trainers**: direct drive (Wahoo Kickr, Tacx Neo, Elite Justo,
  Zwift Hub/Ride, JetBlack); wheel-on is cheaper; rollers for the pure.

## 10. Safety kit and rules of thumb

- **Helmets**: CPSC standard in the US, EN 1078 in the EU. **MIPS**, WaveCel
  (Trek), Koroyd, SPIN (POC) are rotational-impact liners. Virginia Tech
  ratings are the independent test. Replace after any crash. Aero road
  helmets (Evade, Kask Utopia), TT helmets banned from mass-start races in
  2026.
- **Lights**: daytime running lights measurably cut crashes (Trek/Bontrager
  "ABC" study origin). Rear radar (Garmin Varia, Trek CarBack) plus a rear
  light is the 2020s standard for road.
- **Tire pressure**: use a calculator (Silca, Zipp, SRAM). 28 mm road tire
  at 70 kg rider ≈ 70–75 psi; gravel 40 mm ≈ 30–35 psi; MTB 2.4" ≈ 20–25 psi.
- **Torque**: carbon parts need a torque wrench; 5 Nm is the usual stem/seat
  clamp number.
- **Chain wear**: replace at 0.5% (11/12-speed) with a checker, or the
  cassette goes with it.
- **Cleats**: replace worn cleats; knee pain often starts there.
- **Heat** (Arizona): pre-cool, ride before 8 a.m., 1 bottle per 30–40 min
  with sodium (500–1000 mg/L), stop at heat-exhaustion signs (chills, nausea,
  confusion). Heat illness is the local risk, not cold.
