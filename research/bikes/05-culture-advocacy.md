# Culture, advocacy, and cycling for mental health

Evergreen reference. Dated 2025–26 figures are in
[06-current-events-2026.md](06-current-events-2026.md). Health-content rules in
`CLAUDE.md` apply to anything from §5 that reaches a public page.

## 1. Cycling tribes (so copy sounds native)

- **Roadies**: kit, watts, group rides, the "no-drop" vs "drop" ride, the
  shop ride, the Tuesday-night worlds. Rapha/Castelli, shaved legs, coffee
  stop, "half-wheeling" is rude, "on your left," rotating paceline.
- **Gravel**: the 2010s answer to road-racing's ego; privateers, bags,
  "spirit of gravel," Kansas, aero-bar arguments, Radavist aesthetic.
- **MTB**: trail dogs, shuttle days, "sending it," Pinkbike comments,
  enduro, the trail-building volunteer ethic (IMBA), lift-served parks
  (Whistler, Trestle, Highland).
- **Bikepacking / touring / randonneurs**: bags, steel, Rivendell, audax
  brevets, "it's not a race," resupply, ultra dot-watching.
- **Commuters / urbanists**: Dutch bikes, cargo bikes, "bike lanes not
  bike lines," Strong Towns, Not Just Bikes (YouTube), War on Cars podcast,
  Critical Mass (SF 1992), Bike Party, Ciclovía/Open Streets.
- **Fixie / messenger / alleycat**: 2000s NYC/SF, track bikes, Cinelli
  Mash, Red Hook Crit (2008–2018).
- **BMX / dirt jump**: skatepark culture, X Games, the Olympic freestyle
  surprise.
- **E-bike riders**: the fastest-growing group and the most argued-about:
  older riders, families with cargo bikes, delivery workers (NYC ~65,000),
  teens on out-of-class e-motos.
- **Indoor / Zwift**: winter racing, "watopia," the rise of the trainer as
  the main bike for many.
- **Charity / cause riders**: the Cycle for Change audience. Pan-Mass,
  AIDS/LifeCycle (ended 2025), Ride for the Cure, Bike MS, Pelotonia, Tour
  de Cure (ADA), Great Cycle Challenge. Norms: a fundraising minimum, a
  named beneficiary, a jersey, a training plan, a finish-line ritual, and a
  public mile/dollar tracker.

## 2. Etiquette and unwritten rules people actually follow

- Hold your line. Point out hazards. Call "car back." Don't overlap wheels.
  Don't half-wheel. Pull through, then peel off. No aero bars in a group.
- Wave at other cyclists (roadies often don't; gravel and MTB do).
- Yield to uphill riders on trail; hikers and horses have right of way on
  multi-use trails.
- Stop for a rider with a mechanical. Carry a tube, levers, pump/CO2,
  multitool, and a plug kit if tubeless.
- Strava: don't "KOM hunt" on downhills through stop signs; flag dangerous
  segments; private zones near home.
- The Velominati "Rules" (2009) are a joke that some people take seriously.
  Rule #5: harden up. Rule #9: riding in bad weather means you're a badass.
  Don't quote them in mission copy.

**US national**: **League of American Bicyclists** (1880 as the LAW; Bicycle
Friendly Community/Business/University awards, National Bike Summit,
Bike Month in May), **PeopleForBikes** (industry-funded; City Ratings, tariff

Transportation for America**, **Safe Routes Partnership**, **America Walks**,
**Bike Index** (stolen-bike registry), **USA Cycling** (racing federation),
**NBDA** (bike dealers), **BRAIN** (trade press).

**Key US policy vehicles**: **Transportation Alternatives (TA)** set-aside in
the surface transportation bill (~$1.5B/yr), **Safe Streets and Roads for

**Global**: Netherlands (Fietsersbond; 1970s "Stop de Kindermoord"), Denmark

**Arizona / Phoenix**: Coalition of Arizona Bicyclists (statewide),

## 4. Safety facts to cite (with the source to cite)

- US cyclist deaths: ~1,100 per year (NHTSA FARS; 1,103 in 2024, down 6%;
  2025 early estimate up ~4%). Roughly 80% urban, ~30% at intersections,
  ~85–87% male, average age late 40s.
- Protected bike lanes reduce injuries for everyone on the street, not
  just cyclists (Marshall & Ferenchak, *Journal of Transport & Health*,
  2019: cities with more protected lanes had ~44% fewer road deaths per
  capita over 13 years).
- Helmets reduce head-injury risk in a crash (Olivier & Creighton,
  *International Journal of Epidemiology*, 2017 meta-analysis: ~51% for
  head injury, ~65% serious head injury). They don't prevent crashes; the
  infrastructure conversation matters more for population risk. Don't
  moralize about helmets in mission copy.
- Daytime running lights cut crash risk (Danish RCT, Madsen et al.
  2013: ~19% fewer multi-party crashes).
- Rear radar (Garmin Varia) has no peer-reviewed crash study; say "riders
  report" not "reduces."
- E-bike injury numbers rising fast (CPSC NEISS: micromobility ER visits
  roughly doubled 2021–2023); most severe crashes involve cars, not the
  bike itself; battery fires cluster in cheap uncertified packs (NYC FDNY
  data drove the UL 2849 law).

## 5. Cycling and mental health (careful with all of this)

**What the evidence supports saying** (see 06 for the 2024–26 studies with
links):

- Regular aerobic exercise is associated with lower rates of depression and
  anxiety, and structured exercise reduces depressive symptoms with
  moderate effect sizes; the highest-quality trials show smaller effects
  than the headline numbers (BMJ network meta-analysis 2024, Noetel et al.;
  Cochrane exercise-for-depression reviews).
- Cycle commuting specifically is associated with ~20–25% lower risk of
  depression and anxiety in a large UK cohort (Translational Psychiatry,
  2025). Observational, not causal.
- Exercise is a recommended adjunct (not replacement) in substance-use
  treatment (WHO 2024 guidance; 2025 meta-analysis shows moderate effects
  on craving and use).
- Outdoor "green exercise" and social riding show additional wellbeing
  effects in program evaluations (Outride, Cycling UK); those are
  self-reported and uncontrolled.
- Cycling has specific properties people cite: rhythmic, bilateral, low
  impact, outdoors, achievable progress metrics, built-in community, and a
  "flow" state. These are plausible mechanisms, not proven ones. Say
  "many riders describe," not "cycling treats."

**What not to say**: that cycling cures, treats, or replaces therapy or
medication; that it prevents suicide; any effect size without its source;
anything about a specific reader's condition.

**LGBTQ+ context** (Trevor Project 2025 National Survey, published May
2026; 16,667 youth 13–24): 36% seriously considered suicide in the past
year, 40% among trans and nonbinary youth; about 1 in 10 attempted; 62%
report anxiety symptoms, 47% depression; 44% who wanted mental-health care
could not get it. Adult LGBTQ+ figures come from NAMI, SAMHSA NSDUH, and
the Williams Institute. LGBTQ+ adults have roughly double the rate of
substance-use disorder of the general population (SAMHSA NSDUH). Minority
stress (Meyer, 2003) is the standard explanatory framework.

**Crisis lines that go on every mental-health page**: **988 Suicide &
Crisis Lifeline** (call or text 988; press 3 for the LGBTQ+ youth line,
which the federal government cut in July 2025 and Trevor now funds
directly), **The Trevor Project** 1-866-488-7386 or text START to 678-678,
**Trans Lifeline** 877-565-8860, **SAMHSA National Helpline** 1-800-662-4357
for substance use.

**Queer cycling spaces**: OutCycling (NYC; Pride Ride, the largest one-day
queer ride in the US), ALL IN Racing, BikeOut festival (first LGBTQ+ cycling
festival), Trek Ride for Pride rides, Rapha rainbow socks program, Radical
Adventure Riders (formerly WTF Bikexplorers; women/trans/femme/nonbinary
bikepacking), Ride with Pride (various cities), Castro Country Club
Recovery Ride (SF; queer recovery community; 2026 was the first year), LA
LGBT Center's Center Ride Out (LA–San Diego; replaced AIDS/LifeCycle's LA
leg), SFAF's Cycle to Zero. Out pros: Justin Laevens (first openly gay
active pro, 2024), Philippa York, Ayesha McGowan (ally / Black cycling),
several women's WWT riders in public relationships (Kopecky, Moolman
Pasio, etc.). Chris Hoy, Josh Cavallo are not cyclists; don't mix up.

**Black and BIPOC cycling**: Major Taylor (1899 world champion) and the
~100 Major Taylor clubs, Black Girls Do Bike (100+ chapters), Ayesha
McGowan's Thee Abundance Project, Red Bike & Green, Ride for Black Lives,
Justin Williams / L39ION of Los Angeles (crit team), Biniam Girmay

**Women's cycling**: participation gap (women ~25–30% of US cyclists;

## 6. Charity-ride mechanics (relevant to the pledge)

- **Per-mile pledges** ("$0.10 a mile for 10,000 miles = $1,000") work
  because the number is legible and the story updates daily. Platforms that
  support it: Pledge It (Strava-linked), JustGiving (auto-posts Strava
  activities; 31% more raised), DonorDrive activity pledges with a cap,
  GoFundMe Pro (ex-Classy), Givebutter, Raisely. Flat gifts still
  dominate in practice; per-mile is the hook, not the volume.
- **What the successful ones share**: a fixed date range, a live tracker,
  a named beneficiary chosen up front, weekly ride reports with photos, a
  kit, a finish event, matching-gift pushes, and a minimum ask that is
  small and specific.
- **10,000 miles a year** is ~27 miles a day or ~192/week: a serious but
  common target for committed amateurs (a "10k club" Strava badge exists).
  At 15 mph average that is ~13 hours a week. Reporting norms: monthly
  totals, biggest week, elevation, days ridden, "halfway" milestone press.
- The Trevor Project's own **52 Mile Pride Ride Challenge** (Facebook
  mile-logging, 2022: $1.6M+ from 30,000 supporters) is the closest
  precedent for a queer-mental-health mileage pledge.
