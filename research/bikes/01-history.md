# Bicycle history — a working timeline

Evergreen reference. Dates are widely agreed; where historians argue, it says so.
Companion files: [02-brands.md](02-brands.md), [04-racing.md](04-racing.md).

## 1. Before the bicycle (to 1817)

- The "Leonardo bicycle" sketch in the Codex Atlanticus is a 20th-century
  hoax or later addition. Do not cite it.
- The Comte de Sivrac "célérifère" (1790s) is also a fabrication, invented by
  a French journalist in 1891. Do not cite it.
- Real precursors are hobby horses and sledges. Nothing with two inline
  wheels and steering is documented before 1817.

## 2. The running machine (1817–1820s)

- **1817, Mannheim.** Karl von Drais rides his *Laufmaschine* (draisine,
  "dandy horse," "hobby horse"): two inline wheels, steerable front wheel, no
  pedals. You push with your feet. Widely tied to the 1816 "year without a
  summer" (Tambora eruption), when horse fodder was scarce and expensive.
- Patented in Baden 1818; briefly a craze in London and Paris; banned from
  sidewalks in several cities within a couple of years. It fades by the 1820s.

## 3. Pedals arrive: the velocipede / "boneshaker" (1860s)

- **Paris, ~1863–1867.** Pierre Michaux and Pierre Lallement each claimed
  credit for putting cranks and pedals on the front wheel. Lallement got the
  US patent (1866). Michaux & Cie. mass-produced them. Wrought-iron frames,
  wooden wheels, iron tires: hence "boneshaker."
- **1868:** first recorded bicycle races, Paris (Parc de Saint-Cloud, won by
  James Moore). **1869:** Paris–Rouen, the first long city-to-city race.
- The Franco-Prussian War (1870) kills the French industry; the center moves
  to England (Coventry).

## 4. The high-wheeler / "ordinary" / penny-farthing (1870–1885)

- James Starley (Coventry Machinists, then Ariel, 1871) is the key figure:
  tension-spoked wire wheels, larger front wheel for more distance per pedal
  stroke. Solid rubber tires. Fast, dangerous (the "header" over the bars).
- Ridden almost only by young, fit, well-off men. Cycling clubs and the
  first governing bodies form (Bicycle Union 1878, later NCU; League of
  American Wheelmen 1880). The LAW's **Good Roads Movement** lobbied for paved
  roads before cars existed — the road network US drivers use is partly a
  cyclist legacy.
- Tricycles and "safety" variants appear for women and older riders.

## 5. The safety bicycle: the bike we still ride (1885–1900)

- **1885: Rover Safety**, John Kemp Starley (James's nephew), Coventry. Two
  similar-sized wheels, chain drive to the rear wheel, diamond-ish frame,
  rider's feet near the ground. Everything after this is refinement.
- **1888: pneumatic tire**, John Boyd Dunlop, Belfast (Robert Thomson had
  patented the idea in 1845 but it went nowhere). Comfort and speed jump;
  the ordinary is dead within five years.
- Other pieces click into place: diamond frame (~1890), freewheel (late
  1890s), coaster brake (1898), ball bearings, tubular steel.
- **The 1890s bike boom.** Mass production (Pope/Columbia in the US, Raleigh
  in Nottingham 1887/1888). Prices fall; the bicycle becomes a mass-market
  object. Bike infrastructure debates, bike paths, the first bike lanes (e.g.
  Ocean Parkway, Brooklyn, 1894).
- **Women and the bicycle.** The safety bike drove dress reform (bloomers,
  "rational dress") and gave women unchaperoned mobility. Susan B. Anthony,
  1896: the bicycle "has done more to emancipate women than anything else in
  the world." Annie Londonderry's round-the-world ride (1894–95).
- **Race and the bike.** Marshall "Major" Taylor, Black American sprinter,
  world champion 1899, one of the first Black world champions in any sport,
  raced under constant racist exclusion. Major Taylor clubs today are named
  for him.
- The boom busts ~1897–1900 as prices collapse and the automobile arrives.

## 6. The sport takes shape (1890s–1930s)

- Track racing (velodromes, six-day races) is the big money in the US and
  Europe around 1900. Madison Square Garden gave its name to the "Madison."
- Road classics born: Liège–Bastogne–Liège 1892, Paris–Roubaix 1896,
  Paris–Tours 1896, Tour of Flanders 1913, Milan–San Remo 1907, Giro di
  Lombardia 1905.
- **Tour de France, 1903**, created by *L'Auto* newspaper (Henri Desgrange)
  to boost circulation against rival *Le Vélo*. Giro d'Italia 1909 (also a
  newspaper, *La Gazzetta dello Sport*), Vuelta a España 1935.
- **Derailleurs.** Multiple gears exist from the 1900s (Sturmey-Archer 3-speed
  hub 1902; early French derailleurs). Desgrange banned derailleurs from the
  Tour until 1937 as unmanly; riders flipped the rear wheel to change gear.
- Tullio Campagnolo, 1927 (Croce d'Aune pass), frozen wingnuts → invents
  the **quick-release skewer** (patented 1930); founds Campagnolo 1933.
- Cycling becomes working-class transport in Europe and Asia. Bikes as
  military kit in both world wars.

## 7. Postwar: golden age of European racing, decline of the bike as transport (1945–1970)

- **Coppi vs. Bartali** (Italy), Louison Bobet, Jacques Anquetil (first
  5-time Tour winner, 1957–64), Eddy Merckx ("The Cannibal," 1960s–70s, the
  greatest ever by almost any count: 5 Tours, 5 Giri, 1 Vuelta, 19 Monuments,
  Hour Record 1972).
- Campagnolo Gran Sport parallelogram derailleur (1949/1951) sets the
  pattern for modern derailleurs. Campagnolo Record / Nuovo Record (1960s)
  rule pro racing for two decades.
- Tom Simpson dies on Mont Ventoux, 1967 Tour (heat, amphetamines, alcohol).
  Doping controls start the same era.
- In the US and UK the bicycle becomes a children's toy as car ownership
  explodes. Schwinn dominates US kids' bikes (Sting-Ray 1963, "banana seat").
- Dutch and Danish cycling infrastructure is *not* an old tradition: it was
  fought for in the 1970s (Amsterdam "Stop de Kindermoord" — stop the child
  murder — protests, 1971–73, and the 1973 oil crisis).

## 8. The 1970s bike boom, BMX, and the mountain bike (1970–1990)

- **US bike boom 1971–1975**: oil crisis, fitness, environmentalism. Adult
  bike sales roughly doubled. Ten-speed "racing" bikes from Schwinn (Varsity),
  Peugeot, Raleigh, and a flood of Japanese imports (Fuji, Nishiki, Miyata,
  Bridgestone, Panasonic) with Shimano and SunTour parts. Japanese quality
  and value shift the industry's center of gravity to Asia.
- **BMX** grows out of kids imitating motocross on Sting-Rays in Southern
  California (~1970). *On Any Sunday* (1971) spreads it. Boom in the late
  70s / early 80s; Olympic since 2008.
- **Mountain bike**: Marin County, late 1970s. Repack downhill on modified
  1930s–50s Schwinn cruisers ("klunkers"). Joe Breeze builds the first
  purpose-built frames (1977–78); Gary Fisher and Charlie Kelly found
  **MountainBikes** (1979); Tom Ritchey builds frames. **Specialized
  Stumpjumper (1981)** is the first mass-produced MTB. Shimano Deore XT
  (1983). Suspension fork: RockShox RS-1 (1989, Paul Turner). First MTB world
  champs 1990; Olympic 1996.
- **Greg LeMond**: first non-European Tour winner (1986), 1989 win by 8
  seconds using aero bars on the final TT — the moment aero equipment
  became mainstream. 1990 win too. LeMond is also the visible early
  champion of carbon frames.
- Shimano **SIS indexed shifting (1984)** and **STI integrated brake/shift
  levers (1990)** change how every bike works. Campagnolo's Ergopower 1992.
  SunTour, once the biggest, collapses by the mid-90s.
- Clipless pedals: Look 1984 (Bernard Hinault wins the 1985 Tour on them);
  Shimano SPD 1990.

## 9. Materials, aero, and the EPO era (1990–2006)

- Aluminum (Cannondale, Klein, Giant), titanium (Merlin, Litespeed), then
  carbon (Trek OCLV 1992, Kestrel, Giant TCR compact geometry 1997) replace
  steel at the top end. Steel becomes a craft niche.
- Chris Boardman and Graeme Obree's Hour Record battles (1993–96), Obree's
  homemade "Old Faithful" and "Superman" position, and the UCI's 2000 ban
  on non-traditional positions (the "Merckx rule"). The UCI's 6.8 kg minimum
  bike weight (2000) is still in force.
- **EPO era.** Festina affair (1998 Tour). Marco Pantani (1998 Giro–Tour
  double, dies 2004). Lance Armstrong's 7 Tours (1999–2005) stripped in 2012
  after USADA's reasoned decision; he confessed in 2013. Operación Puerto
  (2006), Floyd Landis (2006 Tour stripped). The biological passport (2008)
  changes the landscape without ending doping.
- Mountain biking splits into XC, downhill, freeride (Kranked, North Shore),
  dirt jump, and later enduro (~2013 Enduro World Series). Full suspension
  becomes normal; disc brakes arrive on MTB first (late 90s), road much
  later.
- Bike share: Lyon Vélo'v (2005) and Paris Vélib' (2007) prove the
  large-scale model; Citi Bike NYC 2013.

## 10. The modern bike (2006–2020)

- **Electronic shifting**: Shimano Di2 (2009), Campagnolo EPS (2011), SRAM
  eTap wireless (2015), SRAM AXS (2019), Shimano 12-speed semi-wireless
  (2021). By the 2020s mechanical groups are almost gone from the top end.
- **Disc brakes on road**: UCI trial 2016, fully allowed 2018; the entire pro
  peloton is on discs by ~2021. **Thru-axles**, **tubeless**, **wider tires**
  (23 mm → 25 → 28 → 30+ mm on road) follow.
- **1x drivetrains**: SRAM XX1 (2012) kills the front derailleur on MTB;
  gravel adopts it too. 12-speed MTB (SRAM Eagle 2016).
- **Dropper posts**, **slack/long/low geometry** and **29ers** then
  **27.5"/650b** and **mullet** wheel setups reshape MTB.
- **Gravel** emerges from Midwest US grassroots events (Trans Iowa 2005,
  Dirty Kanza 2006 → Unbound Gravel) into the fastest-growing bike category
  of the 2010s: drop bars, 40 mm+ tires, 1x or GRX (2019).
- **E-bikes** go mainstream in Europe first (Bosch mid-drive 2010/2011), then
  the US. By the early 2020s they are the majority of bike-industry revenue
  in Germany and the Netherlands.
- **Women's racing** rebuilds: Women's WorldTour (2016), Paris–Roubaix
  Femmes (2021), Tour de France Femmes avec Zwift (2022), equal-ish
  Monuments calendar. Marianne Vos, Annemiek van Vleuten, Anna van der
  Breggen, Lotte Kopecky, Demi Vollering, Kasia Niewiadoma.
- **Aero everything**: integrated cockpits, hidden cables, deep wheels, aero
  road bikes (Cervélo S-series, Specialized Venge), then "all-rounders"
  (Tarmac SL7, 2020) as aero and light merge.
- **Strava** (2009), Zwift (2014), power meters (SRM 1986 → Stages 2012 →
  everyone), and heart-rate/FTP culture change how amateurs ride.
- **Sky/Ineos** era in the Tour (Wiggins 2012, Froome 2013/15/16/17, Thomas
  2018, Bernal 2019), then the Slovenian era: **Tadej Pogačar** (2020, 2021,
  2024, 2025, 2026: five Tours, tied with the record) and **Jonas
  Vingegaard** (2022, 2023; Giro 2026). Pogačar's 2024 Giro–Tour–Worlds
  triple was the first since Stephen Roche's 1987.

## 11. Pandemic boom and bust (2020–2025)

- 2020–2021: global bike shortage, record sales, 12-month waitlists. Brands
  over-order; 2022–2024 the industry drowns in inventory. Deep discounting,
  layoffs, bankruptcies and consolidation across brands and retail (see
  [06-current-events-2026.md](06-current-events-2026.md) for the 2025–26 state).
- Tariffs, reshoring talk, and Chinese direct-to-consumer carbon brands
  reshape pricing.
- Road racing becomes more aggressive and more dangerous: faster bikes,
  bigger gears, more crashes. UCI safety body **SafeR** (2023/24) and
  equipment rules on rim depth, bar width, and gearing follow.
- Pogačar / Vingegaard / Evenepoel / van der Poel / van Aert define the
  men's side; Vollering / Kopecky / Niewiadoma / Ferrand-Prévot the women's.

## 12. Ten things to get right when writing about bike history

1. Drais 1817 is the start. Not Leonardo, not de Sivrac.
2. The "safety" (1885) + pneumatic tire (1888) is the birth of the modern bike.
3. The bicycle was a women's-rights machine, and a Good Roads machine, before
   cars.
4. Major Taylor: world champion 1899.
5. The Tour was a newspaper stunt (1903). So was the Giro.
6. Derailleurs were banned from the Tour until 1937.
7. Dutch bike infrastructure was won in the 1970s, not inherited.
8. The mountain bike is a Marin County, late-70s invention; Stumpjumper 1981.
9. LeMond 1989: aero bars, 8 seconds.
10. Armstrong's Tours are vacated 1999–2005; say "stripped," not "won."
