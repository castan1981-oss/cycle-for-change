# Access and speed: the editorial homepage (v7)

**Tested:** `feat/home-editorial-film` at f1c1f0e382 (v7, "the art director's must-fix list"). The branch head is now
1fbc2c3, but the two commits after v7 only add review notes: no file under `cfc-site/` or `scripts/` is newer than v7.
I copied the homepage tree to a scratch folder at 01:26 on Oct 27 and served it from there. The live tree still matched
that copy when I finished (sha1: index.html feb7f92b, home.css 13c6f862, home.js f288c4cf, chrome.css b6f508af,
ridebar.js a79ee614).
**How:** an in-process `http.server` (Range requests on, text gzipped the way Netlify compresses it, `/api/strava` 404)
started from the same Python process that drives Playwright 1.63. I tested in Chromium 153, and in WebKit 26.6, which I
installed for this check: it's the Linux build, not an iPhone. Every request off localhost was aborted. The scripts and
raw results are in `scratchpad/access/` and the screenshots in `scratchpad/access/shots/`. None of it is committed.
**Sizes:** 390×844 first, then 375×667, 360×640, 430×932, 820×1180, 1024×768 and 1440×900. The hero was also checked
at 1280×800 and 1920×1080, and a phone on its side at 844×390. Landscape contrast was read at 844×560 and 932×560,
which draw the same 560px hero.
**Contrast method:** for every line of text, I took a screenshot with the text made transparent and read the background
under each line box. I computed a contrast ratio per pixel against the text's own colour (alpha and opacity blended in)
and scored each line at its 5th-percentile ratio, which is the 95th-percentile background, the same statistic as
`tools/hero-contrast.py`. The hero was read on the poster plus every 0.2 s of the film: 17 frames for each film.
**Focus method:** at every Tab stop, the ring is the set of pixels that change when its outline is removed. I scored
the share of those pixels that reach 3:1 against what they cover, and read the hero's stops on every frame.

## 5. Reduced motion, and the film on phones

**Reduced motion: pass** in Chromium and WebKit.
- With reduced motion on, the film gets no `src` and the poster stays.
- The button reads "Play the film": a press plays it, a second press stops it.
- After a full scroll there are 0 running animations, transitions are 0s and smooth scrolling is off.
- #today's film doesn't autoplay.

**The film on phones: acceptable.** I measured each safeguard:
- **When it starts:** never before load; it starts 0.29 s after it.
- **When it doesn't:** under Reduced Motion, Save-Data, or a 2g/3g `effectiveType` the poster stays. A 4g
  `effectiveType` plays.
- **Under my test throttle:** Chromium reports 4g at 1.6 Mbps / 150 ms, so the film loads.
- **The stop:**
  - It is 48×48 and the 5th Tab stop on phones.
  - A stop holds for the tab session: after a reload there is no film until Play is pressed.
- **Off screen and turning the phone:** the film pauses when under 15% of the hero is visible and resumes when it's
  back. Turning the phone on its side swaps to the wide film.
- **The file:**
  - It is muted with no audio track, `playsinline`, and `moov` is before `mdat` (faststart).
  - It loops every 3.17 s without end, so WCAG 2.2.2 needs the stop, and the stop is there.
- **Flashes:** frame to frame there is at most 1 pair of opposing ≥10% changes in luminance within any second, the
  loop seam included. That is well under WCAG 2.3.1's 3.

**The gap is Robert's phone.**
- Safari on iPhone has no `navigator.connection`. Save-Data, Low Data Mode and a slow connection never stop the film
  there; only Reduced Motion and the button do.
- Every phone visit on cellular costs 805 KB of film after load.
- Low Power Mode is said to block muted autoplay; if it does, the code leaves the poster and the button says Play. I
  couldn't test that without a device.
- If Robert wants the stop to last past the tab: change home.js's `sessionStorage` to `localStorage` (KEY
  `cfc-film-off`). `/privacy/` names the pause memory and would need the same change, the same day.

## 6. Weight and speed: pass

Throttle (Chromium CDP): 4× CPU, 1.6 Mbps down / 750 kbps up, 150 ms RTT, cache off. KB = 1,000 bytes, as sent with
the test server's gzip; Netlify's brotli is a little smaller.

| size | FCP = LCP | LCP element | load event | CLS (load / after a scroll-through) | first load | film after load | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 390×844 | **1.38 s** | `p.hero-intro` | 1.47 s | **0 / 0** | **207 KB** | +805 KB `hero-phone-7.mp4` | **1.01 MB** |
| 375×667 | 1.64 s | `p.hero-intro` | 1.63 s | 0 / 0 | 207 KB | +797 KB | 1.00 MB |
| 820×1180 | 1.67 s | `p.hero-intro` | 1.66 s | 0.0001 / 0.0001 | 228 KB | +805 KB | 1.15 MB |
| 1024×768 | 1.29 s | `p.hero-intro` | 3.31 s | 0.0007 / 0.0008 | 583 KB | +1.36 MB `hero-wide-7.mp4` | 1.95 MB |
| 1440×900 | 1.33 s | `p.hero-intro` | 3.34 s | 0.0003 / 0.0004 | 583 KB | +1.36 MB | 1.95 MB |

- **First load at 390:**
  - HTML 11.2, CSS 23.6 (chrome 15.0 + home 8.6), JS 8.1 (home 6.0 + ridebar 2.1), font 39.4.
  - Images 124.8 KB: poster AVIF 28.1, **#today's video poster 53.4** (M3), asphalt texture 42.1, two dash SVGs 1.3.
  - The two expected 404s, 0.3.
  - Unthrottled, LCP is 0.25 s.
- **The poster isn't the LCP Chrome reports.**
  - Chrome treats an image that fills the viewport as a background. Tested: with the hero at 70vh the poster becomes
    the LCP, at 1.03 s.
  - At 390 the poster has arrived by 1.02 s and paints with the first frame at 1.38 s, so the figure is the same either
    way.
- **At 1024 and up the load event waits on the two story photos** (138 + 174 KB AVIF). Chrome loads lazy images early on
  a slow link. This moves the film's start (3.6 s), not the LCP.
- **The film is one range request.** The phone film starts at 1.74 s and needs about 4 s more at 1.6 Mbps; it plays as
  soon as enough is buffered.
- **CLS:** the only shift is the nav links settling on the font swap at 1024 (0.0007). The lazy pictures, the map and
  the hero don't move.
- **The page's own code:** no library. The page's own CSS is 87 KB raw / 23 KB gzip, its JS 21 KB / 8 KB.
