## X: what still holds the first screen back
1. **Two of him at once on desktop.** In 1440-t1 (tunnel) and 1440-t2 (dusk) a second Robert sits on the left beside the pink shot on the right. 1024-t1 and 1024-t2 do the same. Only t0 and t3 (the empty road beside him) give the spread it should be.
2. **The tunnel shot loses his face.** In 390-t3, 430-t3 and 1440-t1 he is a black silhouette under a hot lamp, soft and noisy. It's the one moment when nobody can tell who this is.
3. **The fisheye, and the crop at 1024.** In the right panel of 1440-t0 the rails and horizon bow like an action cam's. In 1024-t0 his hand runs off the screen edge and Pause the film crowds his front wheel. A smaller one: in 1440-t2 his brake lever lands on the R and the rose plate.

## Y: one line each
1. **His face is under the type.** In 1440-t2 and 1024-t2, FOR runs across his mouth and chin.
2. **The desktop poster has no face.** In 1440-t0 and 1024-t0 there is a drone shot on the left and two riders' backs under a blown white sky on the right. A reduced-motion visitor never sees him.
3. **The forest shot.** In 430-t3, 390-t3 and 1440-t1 it is soft and hazy, with his cream jersey right behind CYCLE: the weakest ground for the letters in either set.

## Against the bar
Y has the better pictures: the mint close-up and the drone shot are a fashion house's quality. X has the better placement and the better sense of place. It reads in five seconds at every size, and it looks like one man's site, not a kit campaign. Y's phone poster is the strongest single frame here, but on desktop it can't carry Y.
