CYCLE FOR CHANGE

Homepage — Final Art Direction Notes for Claude

From: Cadence, Art Direction
Status: Approved direction, final refinement pass
Reference: Deploy Preview 215 and the October 27 review packet

Claude and the Wrenches,

Robert has reviewed the staged homepage and approved the overall creative direction. We’re very close.

The goal of this pass is to recover a little more of the premium, fashion-editorial personality from our approved concepts while preserving the strong production work already completed.

Our creative benchmarks remain MAAP and ASSOS, interpreted through Cycle for Change’s own visual identity.

1. Hero — add “Read the last ride”

Priority: MUST | Phone and laptop

Keep the full-screen moving video hero and existing primary “Ride with me” card.

Add “Read the last ride →” as a quiet secondary text link near “Find a group ride.”

* No additional prominent button.
* Maintain readable contrast over every video frame.
* Preserve the video pause control.
* Ensure the links remain comfortable to use on smaller phones.
* Link to the actual latest ride write-up, not a placeholder.

The hero must remain cinematic and uncluttered.

2. Custom graphics — selective and refined

Priority: SHOULD | Entire homepage

Robert has selected a restrained, premium approach.

Develop a small, cohesive graphic vocabulary using:

* Road-lane markings and painted strokes.
* Editorial numbering and mileage details.
* Subtle diagrammatic route graphics.
* Carefully placed asphalt texture on dark sections.
* The existing rose plates and stencil identity.

Do not add decorative elements simply to fill space.

One excellent custom graphic is better than ten mediocre ones.

3. Storytelling — more editorial composition

Priority: SHOULD | Phone and laptop

The current page is readable and functional, but some sections feel too conventional.

Refine the compositions around “It always says cycling” and “Today I rode for.”

Explore more interesting photography placement, intentional scale changes, asymmetrical grids, and stronger relationships between images and text.

Keep the actual photography clean, with no artificial grading or texture overlays.

Preserve Robert’s existing words.

4. Hero film — refine, don’t replace

Priority: SHOULD | Laptop

The new film is a substantial improvement.

Keep the real footage and existing mobile-first approach.

Review the laptop crops for awkward framing, particularly the low-angle tunnel and palm shots. Make sure the footage feels deliberate and premium throughout the loop.

Do not introduce synthetic footage, distracting transitions, or unnecessary effects.

The phone version should retain Robert as the central visual subject.

5. Mobile visual rhythm

Priority: SHOULD | Phone

Improve the transitions between the story, mileage, calendar, and impact sections.

Introduce more variety through spacing, image scale, typography, and section composition.

Avoid excessive vertical scrolling caused by repetitive text blocks.

Preserve accessibility, legibility, reduced-motion support, and 44px tap targets.

6. Torn-paper captions and bicycle illustration

Priority: NICE

I agree with removing the previous versions.

Do not restore them unchanged.

If the Wrenches can develop a genuinely distinctive graphic motif, present one carefully executed option for review. Otherwise, leave these elements out.

7. Brand and production constraints

Priority: MUST

Preserve the existing:

* CYCLE FOR_ hand-drawn stencil mark.
* After Hours color palette, without introducing new colors.
* Overpass typography.
* Square-cornered buttons and hairline rules.
* Clean photography and real footage.
* SEO content and site functionality.
* Accessibility and reduced-motion behavior.
* External donation links to the supported organizations.

Do not add gradients, shadows, pill-shaped buttons, grain over photography, or generic stock imagery.

Do not invent ride dates, statistics, or campaign copy.

8. Wrenches — final design review

Please review the revised homepage specifically for:

1. Brand fidelity and authenticity.
2. MAAP/ASSOS-level editorial sophistication.
3. Mobile composition and usability.
4. Originality and restraint of custom graphics.
5. Hero-film framing and legibility.
6. Sections that still look templated.
7. Accessibility and performance.

The fact desks should verify any changing statistics, event dates, and organizational claims.

9. Deliverables

Please return:

* An updated staging preview.
* Phone and laptop screenshots.
* Updated hero-film captures if the crop changes.
* A concise summary of design changes.
* Wrenches’ findings and any unresolved issues.
* Confirmation that existing SEO and functionality remain intact.

Do not merge or deploy to production without Robert’s approval.

Creative standard

The finished homepage should feel like a premium cycling editorial experience with a deeply personal story.

Confident, artistic, expressive, slightly feminine, and unmistakably Cycle for Change.

Less decoration. More intention.

— Cadence
