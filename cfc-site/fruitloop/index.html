The A queer night ride in Phoenix

First Friday of every month, starting Friday, December 4, 2026. About 24 flat miles from the rainbow crosswalk at Central and Portland, past eight places where Phoenix’s queer history happened. Free. Nobody gets dropped.

Next ride

Fri, Dec 4

Meet 6:30 pm. Roll 7:00. Back about 9:45.

Add it to your calendar See the eight stops

Eight stops on one loop

Downtown first, where the first Pride march ran, then the west side, the old neighborhoods, Melrose, and home along the bars the ride is named for. At each stop someone tells the story. Tap a number.

  1. 1

    Start2018

    The crosswalk

    Central Ave and Portland St

    The city painted this crosswalk in July 2018, one of two, and it was redone in 2024. It marks Roosevelt as Phoenix’s first gayborhood. Half a block south, at 1011 N Central, Cruisin’ Central opened in 1977. You went in through the alley.

  2. 2

    Mile 21975

    Old City Hall

    125 W Washington St

    In 1975 Sam Burnett and Tony Secuya got a marriage license here, at the old courthouse and city hall, and married in their apartment at 845 N 2nd Ave. A judge voided it. On October 17, 2014, Arizona’s ban fell, and that weekend hundreds of couples married at Heritage Square, which we rode past a mile back.

  3. 3

    Mile 3.51981

    The Capitol

    1700 W Washington St

    Phoenix’s first Pride week was in June 1977. In 1981 somewhere between 600 and 1,000 people marched from Patriots Square, where CityScape is now, down Washington Street to here. We just rode their route.

  4. 4

    Mile 5.51906

    Greenwood Memory Lawn

    719 N 27th Ave

    Nicolai de Raylan came to Arizona in 1906 with tuberculosis and died a few months later. The people who prepared his body called him a woman, and he was buried in a dress with no headstone. In 2019 Marshall Shore, Erica Keppler and the Arizona TransAlliance put one up. It doesn’t out him. It reads: “To be yourself in a world that is constantly trying to make you something else is the greatest accomplishment.” The gates are shut at night, so we stop on 27th Avenue.

  5. 5

    Mile 152018

    The Curve

    7th Ave and Glenrosa Ave

    Phoenix is a grid of straight lines. Seventh Avenue bends here, on the mile of Melrose with more LGBTQ+ bars than anywhere else in Arizona, and the city painted the second rainbow crosswalk right in the bend.

  6. 6

    Mile 15.51984

    Charlie’s

    727 W Camelback Rd

    John King and Kenny Cunitz opened Charlie’s in 1984: a country bar with Arizona’s longest-running drag show. We regroup outside, and nobody has to go in. Look at the route: up 7th Avenue to Charlie’s and back down. The loop has a stem.

  7. 7

    Mile 17Since 2003

    Steele Indian School Park

    300 E Indian School Rd

    Phoenix Pride has set up here every year since 2003. In 1999 Monica Helms, a Navy veteran, designed the transgender pride flag at her home near Indian School Road and 3rd Avenue, a few blocks west of here. It flew for the first time at Phoenix Pride in 2000.

  8. 8

    Mile 231940s to 1990s

    The Fruit Loop

    Roosevelt, 3rd, Portland and 1st

    From the mid-1940s to the mid-1990s these four streets were the Fruit Loop: the bars, the drag stages, the house parties. In 1964 police raided the Eighth Day Coffee House at 915 N 1st St, now an empty lot, and arrested 23 people. The paper printed their names. Around the corner, a plaque at 222 E Roosevelt marks the 307 Lounge, where drag performers got ready across the alley and ran in through the side door.

A drawing of the loop, not a map to ride by. Robert rides it before the first night, and the route file with every turn goes up here after that.

On the way, without stopping

  • Mile 1.2 Heritage Square, where hundreds married the weekend Arizona’s ban fell in 2014
  • Mile 1.9 Patriots Square, now CityScape, where the 1981 march began
  • Mile 10 Encanto Park: water, and the quiet streets of Palmcroft and Willo
  • Mile 19 Cruisin’ 7th, 3702 N 7th St, where Cruisin’ Central moved in the early 2000s
  • Mile 21 Coronado, where gay men bought and fixed up the old houses in the 1970s

How it works

  • Bring two lights. White on the front, red on the back. It’s dark by 5:30 in winter, and Arizona law wants both.
  • Nobody gets dropped. About 10 to 12 mph, flat. A leader up front sets the pace, and a sweep rides at the back.
  • We stop at every red. The front waits on the other side for whoever caught it. Never more than two across.
  • No sticker, no photo. Want to be in the pictures? Take a rose “Photos OK” sticker at the start. Nobody else is in any photo or video we post, and we never post who came.Photos
    OK
  • Nobody drinks on the bike. Free, any bike that can do 24 miles, 18 and up, no sign-up. Just come.

Why it’s called the Fruit Loop

From the mid-1940s to the mid-1990s, Phoenix’s gay bars and drag clubs sat on a few blocks of Roosevelt, 3rd Street, Portland and 1st Street, right where this ride starts. People called that circuit the Fruit Loop. Most of it is gone. The ride ends by riding it.

Phoenix historian Marshall Shore mapped it as a walking tour, and most of the stories on this ride come from his work. This is the same history at bike speed, after dark.

Why the photo rule matters: in 1964 the paper printed the names of the people arrested at 915 N 1st St. Plenty of people still can’t be out at work or at home.

The basics

When
The first Friday of every month, starting Fri, Dec 4, 2026. Meet 6:30 pm, roll 7:00 pm, back about 9:45.
Where
The rainbow crosswalk at Central Ave and Portland St, Phoenix. It starts and finishes there.
How far
About 24 miles, flat, about 10 to 12 mph, with eight stops.
Who
Queer and trans riders, and anyone who wants to ride with us. 18 and up.
Cost
Free. No sign-up.
Bring
A bike that can do 24 miles, a white front light, a red rear light, water.
Host
Robert Castan, Cycle for Change. Questions: a DM to @cycl_eforchange.
First group ride?
It’s flat and it stops a lot, so it’s a good one to start with. First group ride? Start here.
