The Fruit Loop: a queer night ride in Phoenix

A free, no-drop social ride on the first Friday of every month, starting Friday, December 4, 2026. About 13 flat miles at about 10 mph, from the rainbow crosswalk at Central Avenue and Portland Street, past eight places where Phoenix’s queer history happened.

  • When: The first Friday of every month. The first one is Fri, Dec 4, 2026. Meet 6:30 pm, roll 7:00 pm, back about 8:45.
  • Where: The rainbow crosswalk at Central Ave and Portland St, Phoenix. It starts and finishes there.
  • How far: About 13 miles, flat, about 10 mph, with stops.
  • Who: Queer and trans riders, and anyone who wants to ride with us. 18 and up.
  • Cost: Free. No sign-up. Just come.
  • Bring: Any bike. A white light on the front, a red light on the back. Water.
  • Host: Robert Castan, Cycle for Change. Questions: a DM to @cycl_eforchange.

Why it’s called the Fruit Loop

From the mid-1940s to the mid-1990s, Phoenix’s gay bars and drag clubs sat on a few blocks of Roosevelt, 3rd Street, Portland and 1st Street, right where this ride starts. People called that circuit the Fruit Loop. Most of it is gone. The ride ends by riding it.

The eight stops

The city painted two of them: the rainbow crosswalks of 2018, one at the start and one in the middle of the Melrose District. At most of the others we stop for a couple of minutes and someone tells the story.

1. The crosswalk, Central and Portland

The Phoenix City Council approved two rainbow crosswalks in April 2018, and this one went down that summer. It marks Roosevelt as Phoenix’s first gayborhood. Half a block south, at 1011 N Central, Cruisin’ Central opened in 1977. You went in through the alley.

2. Coronado

In the 1970s, gay men moved into Coronado, between 7th and 16th streets and Thomas and McDowell, and fixed up the houses. It was the first Phoenix neighborhood people called gay. We ride through it without stopping.

3. Steele Indian School Park

Phoenix Pride has set up here every year since 2003. Phoenix’s first Pride week was in June 1977, and the first march, in 1981, went from Patriots Square downtown to the State Capitol. In 2000 the transgender pride flag, made in Phoenix by Monica Helms the year before, flew at Phoenix Pride for the first time.

4. Charlie’s, 727 W Camelback

John King and Kenny Cunitz opened Charlie’s in 1984: a country bar with Arizona’s longest-running drag show. We regroup outside. Nobody has to go in.

5. The Curve, 7th Ave and Glenrosa

Phoenix is a grid of straight lines. Seventh Avenue bends here, on the mile between Indian School and Camelback that has more LGBTQ+ bars than anywhere else in Arizona. The city painted the second rainbow crosswalk right in the bend.

6. Encanto Park

No history lesson at this one. Water, the lagoon, three miles to go.

7. 845 N 2nd Ave

In 1975, Sam Burnett and Tony Secuya got a marriage license from Maricopa County and married in their apartment here, at the Westminster. A judge voided it. Same-sex couples in Arizona could marry from October 17, 2014.

8. The Fruit Loop

The last mile is the old circuit. At 915 N 1st St, now an empty lot, police raided the Eighth Day Coffee House early one Sunday morning in 1964 and arrested more than 20 people. The paper printed their names, and people lost jobs and families. Around the corner, a plaque at 222 E Roosevelt marks the 307 Lounge. Across the alley, at 220 E Roosevelt, is the brick building drag performers used as their dressing room.

How it works

  • We meet at 6:30 and roll at 7:00. It’s dark by then from fall to spring, so lights aren’t optional: white on the front, red on the back.
  • Nobody gets dropped. A ride leader sets the pace up front and a sweep rides at the back.
  • We stop at every red light, and wait on the other side for anyone who caught it. Nobody runs a light to keep up.
  • Nobody drinks on the bike. Roosevelt Row is right there after.
  • Want to be in the pictures? Take a rose “Photos OK” sticker at the start. No sticker, and you’re in no photo or video we post.

Why we’re careful with photos

In 1964 the paper printed the names of the people arrested at 915 N 1st St. Plenty of people still can’t be out at work or at home. So the ride never posts who came, and nobody is recognisable in a photo unless they said yes.

The route

Robert rides the whole loop before the first ride, and the route file goes up here after that, with the turns. Until then, the stops above are the route. The ride’s page in the group-ride list has the next date and a file for your calendar.

First group ride?

This is a good one to start with. It’s flat, slow and short, and it stops a lot. If you can ride a bike for an hour, you can do it. First group ride? Start here.
