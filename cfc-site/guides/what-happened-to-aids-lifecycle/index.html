What happened to AIDS/LifeCycle?

The 545-mile ride from San Francisco to LA held its final edition in June 2025. It ended because costs rose and fundraising fell — not because the need went away. Two successor rides launched in 2026.

For over three decades, AIDS/LifeCycle (and the California AIDS Rides before it) meant seven days, 545 miles, thousands of riders, and more than $349 million raised for the San Francisco AIDS Foundation and the Los Angeles LGBT Center.

In September 2024, both organizations announced the 2025 ride would be the last. The reasons were plain: producing a seven-day moving city for thousands of people got more expensive every year, and post-pandemic fundraising never fully recovered. The final ride rolled out of San Francisco in June 2025.

What replaced AIDS/LifeCycle

Both beneficiary organizations launched their own three-day rides in 2026. The San Francisco AIDS Foundation created Cycle to Zero, San Francisco to the Russian River and back (May 21 to 23, 2027). The Los Angeles LGBT Center created Center Ride Out, Los Angeles to Ojai and back (April 23 to 25, 2027). Shorter, cheaper to produce, easier to train for — and the money goes to the same programs.

One warning. A seven-day San Francisco to Los Angeles ride, Joy Ride CA, is set for September 2027. In June 2026 SFAF and the Los Angeles LGBT Center said publicly that it is not AIDS/LifeCycle and is not affiliated with either of them.

What it means if you rode it — or wanted to

The community didn’t disappear; it redistributed. Training ride groups still meet. The jerseys still show up on every climb in California. And the two new rides need riders who remember what the old one felt like.

Rides end. The reason for riding doesn’t.

If you never got your AIDS/LifeCycle, don’t mourn it — pick a ride from the current list and start there.
